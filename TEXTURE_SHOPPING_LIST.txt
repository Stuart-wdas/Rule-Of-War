RULE OF WAR — TEXTURE SOURCING LIST
23 October 2026

Target: grounded, weathered military environments in muted greens, earth tones,
grey masonry and worn roofing. The fixed camera gives roofs, roads and ground
the most screen coverage. Start with these surfaces before small equipment.

DOWNLOAD FORMAT
- Complete PBR material ZIPs, preferably 2K PNG. 4K ground/roof masters are useful
  when available; 8K packs are unnecessary for this pass. Runtime sizes will be
  chosen separately to keep memory use controlled.
- Base Colour/Albedo, Normal (OpenGL / +Y), Roughness and Ambient Occlusion.
- Metallic for metal packs; Height/Displacement is optional reference material.
- Separate maps are fine. Leave the original names intact; I will convert and
  pack the game's occlusion/roughness/metallic texture (R=AO, G=roughness, B=metal).
- Choose seamless/tileable surfaces. Look for neutral base colour without strong
  baked shadows or highlights. Keep the stated physical repeat size in metres.
- For foliage and decals, retain transparent PNG alpha and any supplied masks.
- Keep the source URL and licence beside each pack, especially for paid assets.

FIRST BATCH — HIGHEST VISUAL RETURN
1. Grass/ground: short mixed green grass, drier sparse grass, compacted earth.
   3 packs. Search: "short grass", "dry grassy ground", "compacted dirt".
   Broad colour variation and exposed soil are useful; avoid giant grass blades.
2. Roads: worn fine-aggregate asphalt and dusty fine gravel. 2 packs.
   Search: "weathered asphalt", "fine gravel ground". Road markings are separate.
3. Concrete: weathered pavement/base apron concrete. 1 pack.
   Subtle stains, small chips, restrained cracks and believable aggregate size.
4. Walls: aged neutral lime plaster/render plus worn brick. 2 packs.
   Plaster can be tinted into the building palette. Visible damage is useful.
5. Roofing: weathered terracotta tiles and dark slate. 2 packs.
   Well-defined tile relief without strong baked lighting; include tile size.

That is ten packs for the first delivery. The first three groups alone will
already cover much of the battlefield. Save extra packs for later if preferred.

SECOND BATCH — VEHICLES, PROPS AND NATURE
6. Corrugated or standing-seam roofing: 1 pack for hangars/base buildings.
7. Matte painted steel: 1 neutral pack with subtle wear, dirt and chipped edges.
   Paint is mostly non-metallic; exposed steel may use a metallic mask.
8. Wood: worn exterior boards and darker structural timber, 1–2 packs.
9. Rocks: weathered grey stone with restrained moss, 1 pack.
10. Tree bark: 1 pack with believable small-scale relief.
11. Foliage: 1 leaf/branch atlas and 1 small shrub/grass-clump atlas, with alpha.
    A matching set of low-poly game-ready plant models is useful if available.
12. Uniform fabric and rubber: 1 muted woven cloth and 1 worn rubber pack.
    Unit-specific uniforms/camouflage will still need suitable UV layouts.

THIRD BATCH — THE DETAILS THAT JOIN THE SCENE TOGETHER
- Transparent decal sheets: dirt deposits, soot/scorch, cracks, chipped masonry,
  damp marks and restrained oil staining. Separate masks are useful.
- Rubble surfaces: broken brick, rough concrete and fractured stone.
- Optional trim sheet: window/door frames, gutters, sills and small metal trims.
- Optional vehicle wear masks: dust, scuffs and paint chips rather than a full
  livery for an unrelated vehicle. Existing A-10-specific paint is retained.

INTEGRATION NOTES
The current materials folder is largely colour-only PNGs. Surface relief and
roughness variation are needed as well as cleaner colour images. The material
pass will tune UV scale, tint, normal strength, surface response and terrain
blending for the shared game/Blender lighting. New packs are source assets;
runtime resolution and mipmaps will be chosen per material.
